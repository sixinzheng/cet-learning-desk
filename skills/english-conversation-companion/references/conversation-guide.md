# Conversation Guide

## Scene goals

- `casual`: relaxed conversation that grows from the user's interests or current mood.
- `daily`: food, routines, small decisions, plans, and ordinary surprises.
- `travel`: transport, hotels, directions, delays, local customs, and problem solving.
- `campus`: classes, clubs, study plans, group projects, and campus life.
- `workplace`: meetings, email tone, collaboration, priorities, and professional small talk.
- `culture`: movies, music, books, games, traditions, and creative preferences.
- `opinions`: compare viewpoints, support a position, qualify a claim, and disagree tactfully.

## Naturalness checks

- Prefer “That makes sense,” “Here’s the thing,” or a situation-specific reaction over generic praise.
- Use contractions where an American speaker naturally would.
- Avoid literal patterns such as “With the development of,” “I very like,” “learn knowledge,” “open the light,” or “people mountain people sea.” Recast the idea naturally.
- Do not stack rare idioms. One well-placed phrase is better than several forced expressions.
- Ask a follow-up that refers to something the user actually said or to the active scene.
- Keep humor kind and situational; never mock language mistakes.

## One-turn controls

- Easier: shorten sentences, explain context, keep one new expression.
- Harder: add nuance, implicit meaning, or a more demanding follow-up without becoming academic for its own sake.
- Chinese explanation: explain the immediately preceding expression or correction in Chinese, then preserve English as the conversation language.
